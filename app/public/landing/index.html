01 The problem

The failure isn’t missing data. It’s contradictions.

Site managers work across a tender, a BOQ, a drawing register, RFIs, submittals, DPRs, permits and a phone full of WhatsApp voice notes. Every system unifies documents. None of them notice when they disagree.

02 How it works

Voice is the safety gate, not a transcription box.

One loop, run on every observation — in the time it takes to say it.

  1. 01Speak

    Hinglish, noisy, interrupted. Correct yourself mid-sentence — “C-5… wait, C-6” — and Vesper follows.

  2. 02Extract

    Location, element, attribute, value, drawing and claimed revision become structured fields — C-5 · rebar_spacing · 180 mm · A-102 · R3.

  3. 03Cross-check

    Deterministic rules against the latest For Construction revision, RFIs, BOQ, permits and QA hold points. Not a guess — a rule.

  4. 04Challenge & confirm

    A spoken Hinglish challenge via Rime TTS. You decide — log the observation or raise an RFI — linked to the verified drawing.

08 FAQs

Questions from the site office.

Straight answers to what engineers and PMs ask first.

How accurate is Hinglish in real site noise?
Vesper is designed and tested for code-switched Hinglish with drill-level background noise and interruptions. It doesn’t need a perfect transcript to be safe: for critical fields — drawing, grid location, dimension — it reads back what it understood and asks again when confidence is low.
What happens when the agent is unsure?
It refuses to log. If a drawing number, location or value is ambiguous, Vesper asks one targeted question — “A-102 ya A-109?” — instead of guessing. Nothing critical is written without confirmation, and every contradiction gets a spoken clarification.
Where does the project data come from?
From your own record: tender and BOQ, the drawing register with revisions, RFIs, submittals, DPRs, permits and QA/QC checklists. Template structure comes from InfraLens-style libraries — 550+ Formats, QA/QC and PMC templates referenced to IS and CPWD — so Vesper knows what each log needs.
Does it replace Procore or Aconex?
No. Your document system stays the system of record. Vesper is the voice layer on top that checks field observations against that record before they’re logged. Enterprise plans add ERP integration.
What about offline or low connectivity?
Connectivity on site is patchy, so Vesper fails safe: if a cross-check can’t run, it tells you instead of silently logging an unverified observation. The pilot needs a mobile data connection; for sites with strict network constraints, Enterprise supports on-prem deployment.
How is our data kept private?
Project data is scoped to your organisation and sites. Enterprise adds SSO and on-prem deployment so drawings, RFIs and field records stay inside your infrastructure.
